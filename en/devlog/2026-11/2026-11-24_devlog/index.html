Auto-Canceling Unfilled Orders and Handling Stale Prices for No-Volume Names - First Live Trigger

2026-11-24

I reworked the execution layer so that orders accepted but never filled get auto-canceled after about two minutes, and so names with no trading (and thus stale quotes) don't get orders sent at an old price. It fired for the first time in live trading today, and I also fixed a report bug that mistook "accepted" for "filled."

This is the English version of a post originally written in Korean for my algorithmic trading system devlog(new tab).

Orders that get accepted but never fill

Today's focus was the order execution and safeguard layer(new tab).

Once live trading started, I found that orders would get accepted by the broker but then sit there, never fully filling, more often than I expected.

The problem is that this doesn't just end as "oh, didn't buy it." Cash tied up in an accepted order can't be used to buy the next name in line, so the longer an order stays unfilled, the more the planned buys get pushed back wholesale.

Two fixes

First, if an order still isn't filled after a set time (about two minutes) past acceptance, it now gets auto-canceled and retried once.

But I didn't wire the retry logic straight into live trading. I ran it in "record mode" first: it actually does the cancel, but instead of sending the replacement order for real, it only logs what it would have done — so I can watch whether the judgment holds for a few days before promoting it to live execution.

Second is the no-volume problem. A name that has stopped trading can have a last traded price from a long time ago, and sending an order at that "stale price" means getting hung at an odd price or not matching at all.

So before placing an order, the system now checks whether a fresh quote has come in, and if there's no valid quote, it simply doesn't send an order for that name.

The day it fired for the first time in live trading

Around midday today, one name that had only been accepted and never filled got auto-canceled exactly as designed. That was the first time this flow ran on a real account rather than in simulation.

Safeguards come alive one at a time, only when the situation they were built for actually shows up. Confirming that a device I imagined ahead of time works as expected in the real thing is the main work of these early live-trading days.

The report was mistaking "accepted" for "filled"

The same unfilled-order issue surfaced as a bug on the reporting side too.

The daily summary report was listing names that had only been accepted and then canceled as unfilled right alongside the "names bought today." It was treating acceptance and fill as the same thing.

When a name I didn't actually buy shows up as "bought," it misreads that day's performance. I fixed it to exclude canceled acceptances from the bought list and to flag them separately when needed.

Filtered buys and tied-up cash

I also settled how to handle buys that get held back by the trade filter.

I considered whether the cash reserved for a filter-held buy should immediately roll to the next name in line, and decided to leave it as is rather than roll it. Rushing to reassign held cash too would make that day's buy plan drift from its original intent.

As it happened, the few buys held back today all had their reservations released before the close and ended up buying nothing. That lines up with my current stance that intentional cash is fine to leave sitting.

A full day of re-measuring candidates, and most didn't survive

Separate from the execution side, I spent the whole day measuring candidates that might go into the judgment model(new tab) that picks names.

I skimmed the literature on retail investor flows and sentiment to narrow down candidates. Among them, the folk belief that "a bottom is in once retail buying dries up" was tested against a pre-registered standard over a past window and came out statistically rejected. A few signal candidates were re-run through careful simulation but failed to clear the adoption bar.

Settings like weights and holding periods got several variants recomputed too, and the conclusion was: keep things as they are. Either the evidence that changing them earns more was weak, or much of the apparent gain was an illusion coming from lower trading costs rather than a real edge.

Pre-registering a measurement before seeing its result is what lets me make a "don't change it" call with confidence. It was a day of re-measuring everything and deciding, mostly, to leave the existing settings untouched.
